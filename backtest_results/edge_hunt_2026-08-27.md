# Edge hunt — what is left after M3/M3b/M3c

**Run date:** 2026-08-27
**Status:** exploratory, NOT pre-registered. Does not reopen M3, M3b or M3c.
**Live trading stays halted** (#1053/#1054) — nothing here changes that.

Three findings. One closes the thesis harder than M3 did. One is a costing bug
that has been silently pricing out the only real edge in the dataset. One is an
instrument that has never been built and is the only thing that can settle it.

---

## 1. The market *encompasses* the model — the last door is shut

M3 asked "is the model better than the price?" (BSS = −0.4123, no). That is a
test of **standalone** skill, and it is not the same question as "does the model
carry any information the price lacks?" A forecaster can be much worse
standalone and still be worth combining — the classic Fair–Shiller /
Granger–Ramanathan encompassing test. If it is not encompassed, a **combined**
forecast beats the price and there is a trade. This test was never run.

Run on the M3 gate's own population, using its own imported loading,
de-duplication, exclusion and resolution code (n = 1128 rows, 349 station-days,
reproducing BSS = −0.4187 against the gate's −0.4123 — the small gap is
`allow_network=False`):

```
logit P(YES) = a + b·logit(p_market) + c·logit(p_model)

  a = +0.0311
  b = +1.0373     market weight
  c = +0.0125     model weight   ← the test
```

- `c` 95% station-day block-bootstrap CI: **[−0.0875, +0.1281]**, P(c>0) = 0.59.
  Indistinguishable from zero.
- Adding the model moves in-sample Brier by **+0.00014** — nothing.
- **Out of sample** (fit 08-06→08-16, test 08-17→08-27) adding the model makes
  the combined forecast **worse**: −0.00024.

Note `b ≈ 1.04`, `a ≈ 0.03`: **the market needs no recalibration.** It is already
essentially perfectly calibrated on the logit scale.

**Conclusion.** The model contains no orthogonal information. This is strictly
stronger than "BSS < 0" and it removes the last construction under which a
BSS-negative model could still have supported a trade. The shutdown decision is
correct. The forecast-stack pivot (ΔBSS = −0.0031) already showed better model
*stacks* do not help either; this shows better *combination* does not either.
**Stop trying to forecast the weather better than this market.**

## 2. There IS a real edge in the data — and the repo's fee model hides it

Ignoring our model entirely, is the *price* wrong anywhere? Scored on the full
de-duplicated in-window population with **no exclusions** (7,018 resolved rows,
638 station-days) — rails included, because the rails are 74% of all brackets
and are exactly where prediction markets classically misprice.

The market is well calibrated everywhere except one place, the 1¢ longshot:

| quoted `yes_ask` | n | observed YES | 95% CI (Wilson) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1¢** | **5,318** | **0.094%** | **0.04% – 0.22%** |
| 2¢ | 225 | 0.89% | 0.24% – 3.18% |
| 99¢ | 280 | 99.64% | 98.01% – 99.94% |

Breakeven at 1¢ is 1.0%. Observed is **0.094% — ten times better than
breakeven.** That is textbook favourite–longshot bias, and it is the single
most populated cell in the dataset.

### The costing bug

`src/strategy/fee.py` models the fee as `max(1.0, 7.0·p·(1−p))` ¢/contract.
Against the official schedule (https://docs.polymarket.com/trading/fees, checked
2026-08-27, `fee = shares × feeRate × p × (1−p)`):

- the **`max(1.0, …)` floor is fabricated** — no floor exists, only 0.00001 USDC
  rounding;
- **`0.07` is the crypto rate.** Weather is **`0.05`**;
- **makers are never charged at all.**

| price | repo model | real taker | real maker |
|---|---|---|---|
| **99¢** | **1.000¢** | **0.050¢** | **0.000¢** |
| 90¢ | 1.000¢ | 0.450¢ | 0.000¢ |
| 74¢ | 1.347¢ | 0.962¢ | 0.000¢ |

At the rail the repo overstates the fee **20×**. The gross edge there is ~0.9¢
— so that one fabricated floor is *exactly* the difference between "no edge" and
"edge". (M3b's EV table used **no** fee term at all, and its FAIL verdict came
from a "positive in ≥3 of 5 buckets" rule, not from EV.)

`trades.actual_fee_cents` does not rescue this: every writer stores
`estimate_fee_cents(...)`, i.e. the model's own output. **No fee has ever been
measured.**

### Strategy: buy NO at the 99¢ rail (sell the 1¢ longshot)

Real fee applied; 95% CI is a **station-day block bootstrap** (brackets within a
day share one daily high):

| | mean PnL/contract | 95% block CI | max drawdown |
|---|---|---|---|
| taker | **+0.857¢** | [+0.763, +0.932] | 200¢ (2.0 contracts of stake) |
| maker (0 fee) | **+0.906¢** | [+0.812, +0.981] | 195¢ (2.0 contracts) |

Robustness — stable everywhere:

| split | n | adverse | mean PnL |
|---|---|---|---|
| all days | 5,318 | 5 (0.094%) | +0.857¢ |
| 1st half | 2,666 | 0 (0.000%) | +0.951¢ |
| **2nd half (holdout)** | **2,652** | **5 (0.189%)** | **+0.762¢** |
| excl. today | 5,075 | 2 (0.039%) | +0.911¢ |

**The tail is unusually benign.** The 5 adverse events fall on 5 *distinct*
station-days — never two in one day. Structurally that is forced: exactly one
bracket per station-day resolves YES, so a station-day can lose at most one rail
bracket while winning ~7 others. This is not the correlated blow-up that
penny-picking strategies usually carry. Adverse station-day rate: 5/638 = 0.78%.

The edge also survives away from settlement (so it is not a final-poll artifact):
at 120–240 min out, 4 adverse in 10,687 polls, +0.913¢/contract.

## 2b. …but it is probably OUR capture bug, not the market's mispricing

Framed as a ladder, the same edge is a dutch book. The 11 brackets of a
station-day are mutually exclusive and exhaustive, so buying every NO leg costs
`Σno_ask = 1100 − Σyes_ask` and returns `10 × 100`:

    dutch-book profit = Σyes_ask − 100

Observed median `Σyes_ask` = **109¢** (10 legs quoted 1¢ + 1 leg quoted 99¢), so
the median ladder shows a **+9¢** book — which is just §2's +0.857¢/contract ×
11 legs. Same edge, seen whole. 1.07% of ladders sum below 100.

That 9¢ is **exactly 10 legs × the 1¢ minimum** — which is the tell.

`scanner.py:235-237` stores `max(1, min(99, round(p·100)))`. **Any true price
below 1.5¢ is stored as `1`.** And Polymarket's tick
[tightens to $0.001 when price > 0.96 or < 0.04](https://polyarb-navy.vercel.app/glossary/tick-size)
— precisely this region. So the market is free to quote 0.1¢ on these legs, and
we would record every one of them as 1¢.

Decomposing the overround against what the true leg price might be:

| true longshot leg | true `Σyes_ask` | dutch book | per-contract edge |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1.0¢** | 109¢ | +9.0¢ | **+0.818¢** ← matches the measured +0.857¢ |
| 0.5¢ | 104¢ | +4.0¢ | +0.364¢ |
| 0.3¢ | 102¢ | +2.0¢ | +0.182¢ |
| **0.1¢** | 100¢ | **0.0¢** | **+0.000¢** |

**The measured edge is reproduced only if the true ask really is ~1¢.** If the
market quotes these at 0.1¢ — which the tick schedule explicitly permits and
which a 0.094% outcome rate would justify — the edge is exactly zero and the
entire "9% overround" is an artifact of our own clamp.

Given §1 showed the market is near-perfectly calibrated everywhere it can be
measured (`b = 1.037`, `a = 0.03`), **the base case is that it is also correctly
priced here, and §2 is a measurement bug in our capture rather than an edge.**

## 3. The blocker: this project has never recorded an order book

**The prices above are not executable quotes.** Across 131,435 in-window rows,
`yes_ask + no_ask == 100` on **99.34%** of them. A real two-sided book always
sums to *more* than 100 — you pay a spread on both sides. These are a single
Gamma `outcomePrices` mid, split into complements, then run through
`max(1, min(99, round(p·100)))` (`scanner.py:235-237`).

Two consequences, both fatal to acting on §2 today:

1. **The clamp.** Any true NO price in [0.985, 1.000] is stored as `99`. If the
   real price is 99.9¢ the edge is 0.1¢, not 0.9¢.
2. **No spread.** The executable ask is the mid *plus* half the spread. The whole
   edge is **0.86¢ — less than one 1¢ tick.** A single tick of spread erases it,
   and on illiquid weather brackets the spread is plausibly wider than that.

So: the statistical edge is real and robust; it sits entirely inside an execution
uncertainty that **no data this project has ever collected can resolve.**

### The fix is small and already half-built

`src/data/polymarket.py` already has `get_orderbook()` and
`fetch_orderbooks_batch()` — used at *execution* time (`order_executor.py:45`,
`live_trader.py:118`) and **never at scan time**. Log top-of-book bid/ask and
depth into `bracket_evals` on every poll. That is a contained change to an
existing call path.

With ~2–3 weeks of book data you can answer, with real numbers rather than
argument:

- what is the true best NO ask when the mid clamps to 99?
- how many contracts rest there? (capacity is entirely unknown; live fills only
  ever traded ~€4.43 average, max €5.00 — no capacity was ever discovered)
- does a **resting maker** order at the rail get filled? Maker pays zero fee and
  crosses no spread, which is the natural implementation and the only version
  where the measured edge survives intact.

## The whole thing reduces to ONE measurement

Everything above collapses to a single unknown: **what is the true sub-penny
bid/ask on a longshot leg?**

- If it is genuinely ~1¢ → there is a real, robust, model-free edge worth
  ~0.9% per contract-cycle, and the next question is capacity.
- If it is ~0.1¢ → there is nothing, the market is efficient here too, and
  `edge_hunt` §2 is our own clamp reflected back at us.

Nothing else needs deciding until that number exists. It cannot be recovered
from any archive — `bracket_evals` has been storing the clamped value since day
one — so it requires forward capture.

**Honest prior: I expect this to die.** A 0.9%-per-day model-free return does not
sit unexploited in a liquid venue, and §1 showed this market is well calibrated
wherever we can actually see it. The value of the measurement is that it is
cheap and it is decisive, not that it is likely to pay.

## What I would and would not do

**Do not** spend another day on forecast skill. §1 closes it: the market
encompasses the model, out of sample, on the gate's own population. Better
stacks, better σ, EMOS promotion — all already measured at ~zero, and none of
them can beat an encompassing coefficient of 0.01.

**Do** fix `src/strategy/fee.py` regardless of what happens next — it is wrong
on the rate, wrong on the floor, and it silently prices out the only positive-EV
population in the dataset. Rename `actual_fee_cents`, which has never held an
actual fee.

**The one open question worth instrumenting** is execution at the rail, not
prediction. It is a microstructure question — "can I buy NO at 99¢ in size?" —
and it is cheap to answer with code that mostly exists.

**Regime caveat, stated plainly.** Three weeks, all mid-summer. Longshot bias at
the rail is a bet that brackets far from the current temperature stay far. A
transitional or volatile season could raise the adverse rate materially, and
0.094% has very little room before 1.0% breakeven eats it.
